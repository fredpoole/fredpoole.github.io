Game Dialogue Explorer▌

Pick a game, filter characters by gender, and compare who talks, how much, and how. Built on the Video Game Dialogue Corpus, plus any scripts your class adds.

Show characters coded as

Gender coding comes from the corpus authors, or from whoever added the script. "Neutral" covers unknown, mixed or non-gendered speakers (crowds, narrators, machines).

Rates for characters with only a few lines are noisy. Characters below this size are left out of per-character rate tables.

Anywhere to start

  • Video Game Dialogue Corpus website the games already in this tool, with notes on their sources
  • GameFAQs search a game, open its Guides list, and look for entries titled "Script" or "Game Script"
  • Fandom wikis many game wikis have quest pages with a Dialogue or Transcript section; search "[game] wiki transcript"

Grand Theft Auto V

  • Grand Theft Wiki: Missions in GTA V many missions have a Script page, for example Prologue/Script

  • Red Dead Wiki mission pages; some include dialogue

Cyberpunk 2077

  • Cyberpunk Wiki quest and character pages
  • Fextralife walkthrough quest summaries and dialogue choices, not full scripts

Baldur's Gate 3

  • bg3.wiki: Dialogue links to companion banter, location remarks and the wiki's transcripts category
  • Already in this tool with 38 coded characters. Add a fuller script to go further.

Disco Elysium

  • Disco Elysium conversations (Hugging Face) community export of about 1,700 lines with "actor" and "dialogue" fields, which this tool reads directly; marked all rights reserved, so use it for analysis only

Genshin Impact

  • GenshinDialog extracts every character conversation (121,000+ lines, game version 3.1) from community datamined game files; needs Python, and the output may need reshaping to one speaker and line per row
  • Genshin Impact Wiki: Quests quest pages with dialogue; in game, Paimon menu › Archive › Travel Log replays finished quests

Where Winds Meet, and other new games

  • PCGamesN quest list no full transcripts exist yet; use this to choose scenes
  • Transcribe a few scenes yourself, one line per speech in the form NAME: what they say, and mark the script as a sample. Turning on subtitles, or the transcript on a cutscene video, makes this faster.

Fan transcripts can have gaps and mistakes. Note where each script came from and how much of the game it covers, and treat game dialogue as copyrighted: use it for analysis, and don't republish whole scripts.

Data and credit. Built on The Video Game Dialogue Corpus by Seán G. Roberts, Stephanie Rennick and contributors (doi 10.5281/zenodo.16994468), used under CC BY 4.0. This tool reformats the corpus's per-character statistics and keyness tables and merges some of its gender groups (neutral, genderless and ambiguous become Neutral). It is not made or endorsed by the corpus authors.

If you use the data, please cite: Rennick, S. & Roberts, S. G. (in press). The Video Game Dialogue Corpus. Corpora 19(1). · Rennick, S., Clinton, M., Ioannidou, E., Oh, L., Clooney, C., E. T., Healy, E. & Roberts, S. G. (2023). Gender bias in video game dialogue. Royal Society Open Science 10(5).

Sentiment. Positive and negative words come from the VADER lexicon (Hutto, C. J. & Gilbert, E. (2014). VADER: A parsimonious rule-based model for sentiment analysis of social media text. ICWSM; MIT licence). Emotion words (joy, trust, anticipation, surprise, fear, sadness, anger, disgust, the eight emotions in Plutchik's wheel) come from this tool's own short list of everyday words. Both count single words and ignore context such as "not happy".

Games added through this tool keep only word counts and statistics, never the script text. Full-script search runs on a file you load, and that file never leaves your browser. Game titles and dialogue belong to their publishers.
